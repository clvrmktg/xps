Perishable goods move on a different timeline than general cargo.

For many imports, a short delay may be inconvenient. For perishables, the same delay can affect product quality, shelf life, or even result in complete loss of the shipment.

This is why perishables and cold chain logistics require a different level of coordination.

In The Bahamas, importing temperature-sensitive goods involves more than customs clearance alone. Timing, handling, storage conditions, and transportation all become part of the process.

## What are perishables?

Perishable goods are products that can deteriorate if not stored or transported under proper conditions.

This may include:

- Fresh produce  
- Meat and seafood  
- Dairy products  
- Frozen foods  
- Flowers and plants  
- Pharmaceuticals or temperature-sensitive products  

Many of these shipments require controlled temperatures throughout transport, clearance, storage, and delivery.

This continuous temperature-controlled process is commonly referred to as the cold chain.

## Why timing matters

Unlike general cargo, perishables cannot remain delayed indefinitely at the port or terminal.

Even where products remain technically usable, delays can reduce shelf life, affect quality, or disrupt supply schedules for businesses expecting delivery.

In practice, delays involving perishables often result from:

- Missing or incomplete documents  
- Delayed manifest availability  
- Permit or approval issues  
- Inspection delays  
- Transportation coordination problems after release  

Because of this, preparation before arrival becomes especially important.

## The importance of coordination before arrival

For temperature-sensitive shipments, much of the work happens before the cargo reaches The Bahamas.

Documents, permits, transport details, and clearance planning should already be in place before arrival.

[Early coordination](/insights/managed-vs-standard-clearance/) helps identify problems before they begin affecting the shipment.

For perishable imports, this may include:

- Confirming required permits or approvals  
- Verifying transport and manifest details  
- Coordinating with freight forwarders and carriers  
- Preparing documentation before arrival  
- Planning transportation and storage after release  

When this coordination happens early, shipments move more efficiently through the clearance process.

## Cold storage and handling after clearance

Clearance is only part of the process.

Once the shipment is released, products often still require temperature-controlled handling and transportation.

In many cases, the challenge is not long-term cold storage, but ensuring that shipments move quickly and efficiently through clearance and delivery before product quality is affected.

This may involve:

- Refrigerated transportation  
- Temporary cold storage  
- Coordinated delivery scheduling  
- Controlled unloading and handling procedures  

Without proper handling after release, the cold chain can still be disrupted even if Customs clearance was completed successfully.

## The role of warehousing and distribution

For some businesses, perishables move directly from the port to their final destination.

Others may require temporary storage or phased distribution depending on inventory volume and operational needs.

[Storage planning](/insights/warehousing-in-the-bahamas/) becomes important where immediate delivery is not practical.

Likewise, [timing and coordination after release](/insights/distribution-and-delivery-moving-goods-after-clearance/) are critical to ensuring products move efficiently to their destination.

With perishables, these operational stages become even more sensitive because delays directly affect product condition.
